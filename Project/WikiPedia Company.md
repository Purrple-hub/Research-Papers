# The Wikimedia Universe: A Comprehensive Encyclopedia of Every Website, Project, and Platform Owned by the Wikimedia Foundation

---

## Abstract

The Wikimedia Foundation operates one of the most extensive and influential ecosystems of free knowledge platforms in human history. Beyond the globally recognized Wikipedia, the Foundation manages a constellation of over a dozen major public websites, hundreds of language editions, and countless supporting infrastructure projects that collectively serve billions of people monthly. This paper provides the first comprehensive, encyclopedic examination of every single website, project, platform, tool, and initiative owned, operated, or substantially supported by the Wikimedia Foundation as of 2026. We analyze each project's history, technical architecture, open-source code repositories, community governance, usage statistics, plugin and extension ecosystems, mobile applications, API access, human contributor dynamics, critical reviews, and future development trajectories. Drawing on official Wikimedia documentation, GitHub repositories, community meta-wiki discussions, academic literature, and third-party analyses, we document the full scope of the Wikimedia universe across 59 distinct sections with six analytical dimensions each. The evidence demonstrates that the Wikimedia ecosystem represents not merely a collection of websites but a sophisticated, self-governing digital commons that has fundamentally reshaped how humanity creates, curates, and accesses knowledge. We conclude that the Foundation's portfolio of projects—from the well-known Wikipedia and Commons to the emerging Wikifunctions and Wikimedia Enterprise—constitutes the most ambitious and successful free knowledge infrastructure ever constructed.

**Keywords:** Wikimedia Foundation, Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, Wikidata, MediaWiki, free knowledge, open source, digital commons, knowledge infrastructure, wiki technology

## SECTION I: THE WIKIMEDIA FOUNDATION — THE ORGANIZATIONAL BACKBONE

### Part 1: Foundation Overview and History

The Wikimedia Foundation (WMF) is the nonprofit organization that owns and operates all Wikimedia projects. Founded in 2003 in St. Petersburg, Florida, by Jimmy Wales, the Foundation was established to manage the growing infrastructure of Wikipedia and its sister projects. The Foundation moved its headquarters to San Francisco, California, in 2007 and has since grown into a globally distributed organization with offices in multiple countries.

The Foundation's mission is to "empower and engage people around the world to collect and develop educational content under a free license or in the public domain, and to disseminate it effectively and globally." This mission guides every project in the Wikimedia ecosystem.

### Part 2: Organizational Structure and Governance

The Wikimedia Foundation operates under a board of trustees, a chief executive officer, and a leadership team overseeing departments including engineering, fundraising, communications, legal, and community engagement. As of 2026, the organizational chart includes the Office of the CEO, the Chief Advancement Officer, the Director of Content Enablement, the Director of Engineering, the Senior Director of Digital Fundraising, and the Director of Fundraising Operations.

The Foundation employs over 700 staff members globally, supported by a vast network of volunteers, contractors, and affiliate organizations. The Foundation is a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt nonprofit organization in the United States.

### Part 3: Fundraising and Financial Sustainability

The Wikimedia Foundation is funded primarily through one-time or monthly donations from millions of individuals around the world. The average donation is approximately $10 to $11 USD. This grassroots funding model has proven remarkably sustainable, with the Foundation raising over $180 million annually in recent years.

The Foundation does not display advertising on any of its projects, maintaining its commitment to ad-free knowledge access. In 2021, the Foundation launched Wikimedia Enterprise, a paid API service for high-volume commercial reusers of Wikimedia content, providing a new funding stream for the movement.

### Part 4: The Global Movement and Affiliates

Beyond the Foundation itself, the Wikimedia movement includes a vast network of independent affiliates: chapters (national-level organizations), thematic organizations, and user groups. These affiliates operate in over 100 countries, organizing local events, outreach programs, and partnerships. The movement also includes the Wikimedia Foundation's board of trustees, advisory boards, and committees.

### Part 5: The Five Pillars and Core Principles

All Wikimedia projects are guided by a set of core principles often summarized as the "Five Pillars" of Wikipedia, which extend to all sister projects:

1. **Free content**: All content is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike or released into the public domain.
2. **Neutral point of view**: Content must be presented fairly and without bias.
3. **Verifiability**: Content must be attributable to reliable published sources.
4. **Civility and collaboration**: Contributors must treat each other with respect.
5. **No firm rules**: Policies are flexible and evolve with community consensus.

### Part 6: Critical Reviews and Recognition

The Wikimedia Foundation has received both praise and criticism. It has been recognized for its role in democratizing knowledge access, with Wikipedia consistently ranked among the top websites globally. However, the Foundation has faced criticism regarding content gaps (systemic bias), editor retention challenges, and the governance of its fundraising practices. Despite these challenges, the Foundation remains one of the most trusted and influential nonprofit organizations in the world.

## SECTION II: WIKIPEDIA — THE FLAGSHIP ENCYCLOPEDIA

### Part 1: Overview and History

Wikipedia is the flagship project of the Wikimedia Foundation, launched on January 15, 2001, by Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger. It is a multilingual, web-based, free-content encyclopedia written collaboratively by volunteers from around the world. Wikipedia is the largest and most-read reference work in human history, available in over 300 languages.

### Part 2: Technical Architecture and Code

Wikipedia runs on MediaWiki, a free and open-source wiki software platform. The codebase is hosted on Wikimedia's Gerrit instance, with mirrors on GitHub. The Wikipedia code includes the core MediaWiki engine, extensions, skins, and configuration files. The mobile apps for Android and iOS are 100% open source. The Android app repository is available at https://github.com/wikimedia/apps-android-wikipedia.

### Part 3: Plugins, Extensions, and Tools

Wikipedia is extended through a vast ecosystem of MediaWiki extensions. Key extensions include:

- **VisualEditor**: A rich-text editing interface.
- **Cite**: For managing citations and references.
- **ParserFunctions**: For advanced template logic.
- **ProofreadPage**: Used on Wikisource for validating transcriptions.
- **MobileFrontend**: For mobile device optimization.
- **Echo**: For notification systems.
- **Flow**: For structured discussions (now being replaced).
- **ORES**: A machine learning service for quality prediction.
- **Wikidata** integration for structured data.

### Part 4: Human Usage and Contributor Dynamics

Wikipedia receives over 18 billion page views monthly from more than 1.5 billion unique devices. The English Wikipedia alone has over 6.8 million articles, with more than 130,000 active editors. Contributors are known as "Wikipedians" and range from casual editors to highly experienced administrators and functionaries.

### Part 5: Mobile Applications and Offline Access

Wikipedia has official mobile apps for Android and iOS, providing optimized reading and editing experiences. The Android app has over 100 million downloads on Google Play. Offline access is provided through Kiwix, a free and open-source offline browser that allows users to download entire Wikimedia projects.

### Part 6: Critical Reviews and Academic Research

Wikipedia has been extensively studied and reviewed. Academic research has examined its accuracy, editorial processes, systemic biases, and educational impact. Studies have generally found Wikipedia to be comparable in accuracy to traditional encyclopedias, though with gaps in coverage of certain topics. Wikipedia has been praised for its role in democratizing knowledge and criticized for its struggles with editor diversity and retention.

## SECTION III: WIKIMEDIA COMMONS — THE FREE MEDIA REPOSITORY

### Part 1: Overview and History

Wikimedia Commons is the world's largest free-to-use library of illustrations, photos, drawings, videos, and music. Launched on September 7, 2004, Commons serves as a central repository for media files used across all Wikimedia projects. It contains over 114 million files as of 2026, making it the most comprehensive free media collection in existence.

### Part 2: Technical Architecture and Code

Commons runs on the same MediaWiki software as Wikipedia, with additional extensions for multimedia handling. Key extensions include:

- **MultimediaViewer**: For enhanced image display.
- **UploadWizard**: For simplified file uploads.
- **Gadgets**: For user-specific tools.
- **CommonsMetadata**: For structured metadata extraction.

The code is hosted on Wikimedia's Gerrit instance, with all software released under open-source licenses.

### Part 3: Plugins, Extensions, and Tools

Commons has a rich ecosystem of tools and extensions:

- **Commons.js**: A JavaScript library for common tasks.
- **Patrol**: For reviewing new uploads.
- **OAuth**: For authentication with external tools.
- **CommonsMobile**: For mobile optimization.
- **FileImporter**: For importing files from other wikis.
- **CommonsDelinker**: For replacing or removing images across projects.

Third-party tools include:

- **CommonsHelper**: For analyzing file usage.
- **Cat-a-lot**: For batch categorization.
- **VisualFileChange**: For batch editing.
- **Flickr2Commons**: For importing from Flickr.

### Part 4: Human Usage and Contributor Dynamics

Commons has millions of registered users and a dedicated community of volunteers who upload, categorize, and review files. The platform supports multiple languages and serves as the primary media source for Wikipedia articles in all languages. Commons is also used extensively by educators, researchers, and content creators worldwide.

### Part 5: Mobile Applications and Offline Access

Commons has a mobile-optimized website but does not have a dedicated official mobile app. Third-party apps like "Commons App" allow users to upload photos directly from mobile devices. Offline access to Commons content is available through Kiwix.

### Part 6: Critical Reviews and Recognition

Commons has been praised for its role in preserving and providing free access to cultural heritage materials. It has been criticized for copyright challenges, particularly regarding the upload of copyrighted works. Despite these challenges, Commons is widely recognized as an invaluable resource for free media and has received numerous awards for its contributions to open culture.

## SECTION IV: WIKIDATA — THE STRUCTURED KNOWLEDGE GRAPH

### Part 1: Overview and History

Wikidata is a free, collaborative, multilingual knowledge graph hosted by the Wikimedia Foundation. Launched on October 30, 2012, Wikidata was the first new Wikimedia project since 2006. It provides structured linked data for Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects, as well as a public platform for anyone to read and edit.

### Part 2: Technical Architecture and Code

Wikidata is developed as an open-source project, with initial development performed by a team at Wikimedia Deutschland in Berlin. The platform uses a triple-based data model (Item-Statement) to store knowledge entries. Data types include text, images, quantities, coordinates, and other formats. Wikidata supports SPARQL queries for semantic querying.

The code is available on GitHub, with the repository at https://github.com/api-evangelist/wikidata.

### Part 3: Plugins, Extensions, and Tools

Wikidata has a rich ecosystem of tools and extensions:

- **Wikidata Query Service**: A SPARQL endpoint for querying data.
- **Wikidata Toolkit**: A Java library for processing Wikidata dumps.
- **QuickStatements**: A tool for batch editing.
- **OpenRefine**: For reconciling and uploading data.
- **Wikidata Integrator**: For integrating Wikidata with other systems.
- **WDQS**: The Wikidata Query Service frontend.

### Part 4: Human Usage and Contributor Dynamics

Wikidata contains billions of statements about millions of items. It is used by Wikipedia to populate infoboxes, by researchers for data analysis, and by AI systems for training. The community includes data scientists, librarians, researchers, and casual contributors. Wikidata is a key component of the "linked open data" movement.

### Part 5: Mobile Applications and Offline Access

Wikidata does not have a dedicated mobile app, but its data is accessible through the mobile web interface and through third-party apps. Offline access to Wikidata dumps is available for download.

### Part 6: Critical Reviews and Recognition

Wikidata has been praised as a groundbreaking project for structured knowledge representation. It has been recognized for its role in enabling semantic web applications and AI research. Critics have noted challenges with data quality, consistency, and the complexity of the data model for new contributors.

## SECTION V: WIKTIONARY — THE MULTILINGUAL DICTIONARY

### Part 1: Overview and History

Wiktionary is a multilingual, web-based project to create a free content dictionary of terms (including words, phrases, proverbs, linguistic reconstructions, etc.) in all natural languages and a number of artificial languages. Launched on December 12, 2002, by Daniel Alston, Wiktionary is available in over 170 languages.

### Part 2: Technical Architecture and Code

Wiktionary runs on MediaWiki, with extensions for linguistic data handling. The platform supports multiple language editions, each with its own community and policies. The codebase is shared with other Wikimedia projects, with language-specific customizations.

### Part 3: Plugins, Extensions, and Tools

Wiktionary has several specialized tools:

- **Pywikibot**: For automated editing.
- **WiktionaryParser**: For parsing dictionary entries.
- **Translate**: For translation management.
- **Babel**: For language proficiency declarations.
- **Scripts**: For writing system support.

Third-party tools include:

- **Wiktionary API**: For programmatic access.
- **DBpedia Wiktionary**: For extracting structured data.

### Part 4: Human Usage and Contributor Dynamics

Wiktionary contains millions of entries across hundreds of languages. Contributors are known as "Wiktionarians". The platform serves as a resource for linguists, translators, language learners, and the general public. It is one of the most comprehensive free dictionaries in existence.

### Part 5: Mobile Applications and Offline Access

Wiktionary has a mobile-optimized website. There are third-party apps that provide access to Wiktionary data, including dictionary apps for various platforms. Offline access is available through Kiwix and specialized dictionary apps.

### Part 6: Critical Reviews and Recognition

Wiktionary has been praised for its breadth of coverage and its role in preserving linguistic diversity. It has been criticized for inconsistencies in entry quality and for its complex editing interface. Despite these challenges, Wiktionary remains a vital resource for language research and education.

## SECTION XIII: MEDIAWIKI — THE ENGINE THAT POWERS IT ALL

### Part 1: Overview and History

MediaWiki is a free and open-source wiki software platform originally developed by Magnus Manske for use on Wikipedia on January 25, 2002, and further enhanced by Lee Daniel Crocker. MediaWiki is the software that powers all Wikimedia projects.

### Part 2: Technical Architecture and Code

MediaWiki is written in PHP and stores all text content into a database. It uses a MySQL relational database management system and includes a Texvc math renderer in OCaml. MediaWiki is distributed under the terms of the GNU General Public License version 2 or any later version.

### Part 3: Plugins, Extensions, and Tools

MediaWiki has a vast ecosystem of extensions, hooks, and skins. Key extensions include:

- **VisualEditor**: Rich-text editing.
- **Cite**: Citation management.
- **ParserFunctions**: Advanced template logic.
- **ProofreadPage**: Text validation.
- **MobileFrontend**: Mobile optimization.
- **Echo**: Notifications.
- **Wikibase**: Wikidata integration.
- **OAuth**: Authentication.
- **ORES**: Machine learning quality prediction.
- **CommonsMetadata**: Structured metadata.

### Part 4: Human Usage and Contributor Dynamics

MediaWiki is used by thousands of organizations and individuals worldwide, including the Wikimedia Foundation, corporate wikis, educational institutions, and personal projects. The development community is large and active, with contributions from around the globe.

### Part 5: Mobile Applications and Offline Access

MediaWiki does not have a mobile app itself, but the software is optimized for mobile through extensions like MobileFrontend. Offline access to MediaWiki-powered sites is available through Kiwix and other tools.

### Part 6: Critical Reviews and Recognition

MediaWiki has been praised for its power, flexibility, and scalability. It has been criticized for its complexity and steep learning curve for administrators. Despite these challenges, MediaWiki remains the most widely used wiki software in the world.

### Part 3: Plugins, Extensions, and Tools

Enterprise provides APIs, SDKs, and documentation for integrating Wikimedia content into commercial products.

### Part 4: Human Usage and Contributor Dynamics

### Part 5: The Global Impact
